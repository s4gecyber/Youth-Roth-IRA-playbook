The three plays, ranked by cleanliness

  • Startup capital ($25K–contributions only) — Take only contributions. Zero tax, zero penalty. The growth keeps compounding.
  • Home purchase ($contributions + $10K) — Use the first-time-buyer exception on $10K of earnings, tax and penalty-free. Once-in-a-lifetime credit.
  • More than that — You're cutting into taxable earnings: 10% income tax + 10% early withdrawal penalty = ~20¢ per earnings dollar. Use sparingly.

Assumptions baked in

  • Beginning-of-year contributions, compounded annually at the rate you set.
  • Retirement age 60.
  • At 18, contributions come out first by IRS ordering rules — no choice involved.
  • "Continues contributing" assumes the same annual contribution amount through age 60.
  • Tax estimate for non-qualified earnings withdrawal assumes 10% federal income bracket + 10% early-withdrawal penalty.
  • Ignores Hawaii / state tax, inflation, and contribution-limit increases (the limit has historically risen ~$500 every 1-2 years).

Why this works (the structural requirements)

  • Your business must be a sole proprietorship or a partnership where the only partners are the child's parents (a spousal LLC taxed as a partnership qualifies). S-corp / C-corp doesn't get the same payroll tax exemption.
  • Wages must be reasonable for actual work performed and documented (time logs, W-2 issued).
  • The Roth IRA must be opened as a custodial Roth IRA for Kids — Fidelity, Schwab, and Vanguard all offer this.
  • The 5-year rule on Roth earnings starts the year of the first contribution — start early to back-date the clock.
